# Product management with Hermes

How an always-on agent keeps product moving without becoming a bottleneck. This
is the operating model, generalized — not tied to a specific ticketing tool.

## The problem it solves

For a small team (or a solo founder wearing the PM hat), the failure mode isn't
*doing* the work — it's the connective tissue:

- Ideas and bugs surface in chat and vanish before they become tickets.
- The backlog drifts out of sync with reality.
- Tickets are vague, so planning becomes interrogation.
- Nobody has time to keep statuses and priorities honest.

Hermes exists to own that tissue so the humans can do the actual product
thinking.

## The ticket lifecycle

### 1. Capture
Anything that should become work — a message, a bug, a feature thought — gets
turned into a ticket *at the moment it appears*, not "later." The bar to capture
is low; the bar to leave it half-formed is high.

### 2. Shape
A raw capture becomes a **well-formed ticket**: clear title, the context needed
to act, and acceptance criteria where they apply. Where information is missing,
Hermes marks the gap explicitly rather than inventing a plausible spec.

### 3. Triage
Incoming items get sorted: priority, type, likely owner, duplicates flagged
against what already exists. The point is that the board reflects reality
without a human babysitting it.

### 4. Keep honest
On an ongoing basis, Hermes surfaces what's **stalled**, what's **unclear**, and
what's **ready** — so a planning session is a quick review of an already-tidy
board, not an archaeology dig.

### 5. Draft the artifacts
User stories, acceptance criteria, release notes — drafted for a human to
review and approve. Drafted, never shipped unattended.

## What stays human

Hermes keeps product *moving*; it doesn't get to *decide* product:

- **Prioritization calls** — it can propose; the human sets the bar.
- **Scope and roadmap** — Hermes organizes, people decide what's in.
- **Anything customer-facing** — release notes and updates are drafts until a
  human signs off.

## Why "well-formed by default" matters

A ticket system is only as useful as the worst ticket in it. The single highest-
leverage thing an agent can do here is make **well-formed the default** —
consistent titles, real context, explicit gaps — so that by the time a human
looks, the work is already legible. That's the difference between a backlog
that's an asset and one that's a graveyard.
